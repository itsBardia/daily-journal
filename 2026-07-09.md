### 📖 Learned

**From KhashayarTalks:**

- **The art of starting** — the hardest part is the friction *before* you begin. Once you start, it becomes simple.  
- **Identity-based thinking** — instead of "I want to lose 5kg," think "I am a healthy person."  
- **Focus on the "why"** — consequences matter more than just benefits. Ask: *What happens if I don't learn English?* *What if I don't exercise?*  
- **Willpower techniques:**  
  - Design your environment  
  - Remind yourself of the "why"  
  - Reframe pleasure and discomfort  
  - Use short-term negative visualization (see the cost of bad habits)  
  - Cognitive distancing (step back and observe your thoughts)  
- **One key doesn't open every door** — what works for others may not work for you. You need to find your own path.  
- **Abstinence vs. moderation** — depends on the habit and your personality. Decide if it's better to quit entirely or practice balance.  
- **Tools for self-control (different from willpower):**  
  1. **Intrinsic motivation** — doing something for the sake of the activity itself, not a reward  
  2. **Psychological distance (Batman effect)** — look at yourself in third person. Instead of "I must study," say "Bardia must study." This reduces pressure and increases curiosity. (Batman experiment: kids in superhero costumes performed boring tasks much better.)  
  3. **Turning failure into curiosity** — instead of saying "I failed," ask: "Where did I go wrong? What habit caused this? What small change could fix it?"  
- **Third-person perspective** — makes us more compassionate, less judgmental, and more curious about ourselves.  
- **Willpower vs. self-control:**  
  - Willpower = resistance and endurance  
  - Self-control = using tools and strategies so that you don't have to rely on willpower as much
